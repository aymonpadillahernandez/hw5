What we expect the team to do

Who the Boss calls first

INInventory

The Boss reads the ticket and the business date, then calls Inventory first. The whole ticket hangs on one question: is a size S tee on the shelf? If it is, this is a simple sale and nothing else matters. If it is not, the linked reprint invoice becomes the way to get one, and that is a money question for Accounting.

Calling Accounting or Customer Service before we know the stock would mean planning a bill payment or writing to the customer without the key fact.

Delegations we expect

    Check CC-TEE-WHITE in size S, list the sizes that do have stock, and name the vendor that reprints apparel and its lead time.

  3. B→CSCustomer Service

    Draft an honest reply: size S is not available today. Offer another size that is in stock now, or size S after the reprint's lead time. Promise no date we cannot confirm.

FAFacilities not needed: no lease, rent or shop space is involved.

What we expect the team to do

Who the Boss calls first

FAFacilities

Facilities goes first because this is a request for money that arrived by email, and a fake rent notice is one of the easiest ways to steal from a small shop. Before anyone talks about paying, Facilities checks the notice against the lease on record: is it the right landlord, the right amount and the right due date? It also checks that this month's rent has not already been paid.

Only once the notice is confirmed as genuine does it make sense to ask Accounting how to pay it.

Delegations we expect

  1. B→FAFacilities

    Verify the notice against lease 1: landlord, monthly rent and next due date. Count the days left from the business date, look for a rent payment already recorded, and flag that an email can't be trusted until it's confirmed with the landlord's contact on file.

  2. B→ACAccounting

    Say whether checking can pay the rent by its due date when the overdue $840 reprint invoice is also waiting, and what balance would be left after each payment.

INInventory not needed: no products are involved.
CSCustomer Service not needed: the landlord is not a customer, so no customer reply is required.

reflection

Reflection

My own evaluation of the official run (the dashboard session on 2026-10-12), compared with the plan in each ticket's Expected section, plus ideas for what the system could take on next. Answers are recorded in my own words, one question at a time.

Ticket 101 · Bulldog tee

Q1How did the agent team perform on ticket 101, and how does what actually happened compare with our Expected plan?

overall good result, but not exactly as expected. I expected Inventory to go first and then involve the other agents depending on what it found. Instead, Inventory and Customer Service worked almost in parallel. They correctly identified that size S was out of stock and proposed buying 1 unit for $8, which I approved. The main miss was that Accounting did not check the linked $840 invoice 501, which was part of our original plan and remained open after the ticket was resolved.

Ticket 102 · Rent due

Q2How did the agent team perform on ticket 102, and how does what actually happened compare with our Expected plan?

this was the cleanest one. Facilities was the right agent and checked the landlord, amount, due date and previous payments. The main difference is that I expected Accounting to also check cash and the other open invoice, but it was not involved. The agents also correctly flagged that the email could not be fully verified inside the system.

Ticket 103 · Bulk hoodie discount

Q3How did the agent team perform on ticket 103, and how does what actually happened compare with our Expected plan?

the agents correctly found that we only had 8 hoodies vs 20 requested and that cost was $22 vs $58 list price. I expected Accounting to propose a bulk price, but the system did not because there was no discount policy or requested target price in the database. I actually think this was a good decision because it avoided inventing a commercial rule. We finally approved purchasing the missing 12 units for $264.

One agent or a team?

Q4What would have been simpler with one agent + tools?

  • ticket 102 for sure. One agent could have checked the ticket, lease, payments and cash without needing a multi-agent workflow
  • ticket 101 probably as well. One agent could have checked stock, invoice, pricing and cash together and maybe avoided missing invoice 501
  • ticket 103 is where the multi-agent setup made more sense because we needed inventory, pricing and customer communication at the same time

New business problems

Q53 new problems we could solve with the tools we already have

  1. a customer asks for a product or size that is out of stock
  2. a vendor asks about an unpaid invoice
  3. another rent notice arrives for the current shop

Q63 problems we could not solve today, and what they would need

  1. define the right bulk discount → we would need a real pricing / margin policy and probably a pricing tool
  2. confirm that a vendor can really supply a specific SKU and at what price → we would need vendor availability / quote tools
  3. verify an external email or contact the landlord/customer directly → we would need email / CRM tools
